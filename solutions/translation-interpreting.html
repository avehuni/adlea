Translation and Interpreting

Expert translation and interpreting services built for the specific language and content demands of Armenian Dual Language Immersion, because educational meaning cannot afford to be lost in translation.

Why This Matters

When Meaning Is Lost, So Is the Program

Translation in education is not a generic task. When instructional resources, parent communications, or programmatic documents are translated by someone who is bilingual but not grounded in the content and context of dual language immersion, the meaning shifts. A term that carries precise pedagogical weight in one language loses that precision when rendered by someone who does not know what that term does in a DLI classroom. The result is not a translated document but an approximated one, and in education, approximation has consequences for teachers, learners, and families alike.

Bilingualism alone does not make an educational translator. ADLEA's translation and interpreting services are provided by professionals who are not only fluent in Armenian and the partner language, but who bring deep familiarity with DLI program structures, biliteracy frameworks, and the specific content areas taught through Armenian, ensuring that every document and every conversation carries the full weight of its intended meaning.

What We Provide

Three Services. One Standard of Expertise.

1

Academic Translation of Instructional Resources

Accurate, field-informed translation of curricula, lesson plans, assessments, professional development materials, and other instructional documents, preserving not just the words but the pedagogical intent behind them.

2

Interpreting for Program Needs

Real-time interpreting support for school board presentations, parent information nights, professional learning sessions, community meetings, and any other programmatic context where language access determines whether key stakeholders can meaningfully participate.

3

Language Access for Armenian-Medium Classrooms

Translation of parent communications, enrollment materials, school-to-home correspondence, and programmatic documentation into Armenian and the partner language, ensuring that families, partners, and community stakeholders are never left outside the conversation.
