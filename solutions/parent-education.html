Expanding Access

Parent Education

Educating parents on the purpose and benefits of DLI for program sustainability.

Why This Program Exists

Armenian Dual Language Immersion is a choice program. Families enroll because they want something more for their children, biliteracy, cultural connection, academic depth. But when content gets complex, as it inevitably does around second and third grade, families who do not fully understand the model are the first to pull their children out. "This is too much Armenian," they say, not because the program has failed, but because no one helped them understand what the program actually is.

Armenian DLI is not a heritage language class. It is not basic literacy or the ability to write a letter to grandparents. It is a rigorous, standards-based immersion program that develops language to the depth a child would have received had they been educated in Armenia, while simultaneously developing full academic proficiency in the partner language of the country where the program is offered. That is an extraordinary educational commitment, and families cannot sustain what they do not understand.

What This Program Delivers

Building the Foundation Programs Depend On

1

Resources to Help Parents Understand the DLI Model

Accessible, well-designed materials that explain what Armenian DLI actually is: how biliteracy develops, what to expect at each grade level, why the early years feel heavy in Armenian, and what the research says about long-term academic and cognitive outcomes. Parents who understand the model become its most powerful advocates.

2

Workshops on the Long-Term Benefits of Bilingualism

Interactive sessions, in person and virtual, that bring parents into the research on bilingualism, biliteracy, and dual language immersion. What does it mean for a child's brain, academic trajectory, and identity to develop two languages at the highest academic level? These workshops answer those questions in language families can connect to and act on.

3

Stronger Programs Through Informed, Engaged Families

Parent education is not a side program. It is infrastructure. When families understand and believe in the model, they stay through the hard years, advocate within their school communities, and become the foundation that allows programs to grow, stabilize, and thrive. Without informed, engaged families, there are no sustainable DLI programs.
